# Delays and cancellations under EU261

## Long delays

A delay entitles the passenger to compensation only when the flight reaches its final destination three
hours or more after the scheduled arrival time. The delay is measured at arrival, not departure. A delay
of under three hours gives no compensation, although the airline may still owe meals and refreshments.
The three-hour threshold comes from the Court of Justice ruling in Sturgeon (C-402/07), which treats a
long delay like a cancellation for compensation purposes.

## Cancellations

A cancelled flight gives the right to compensation unless the airline can show one of the following:

- It told the passenger at least 14 days before departure.
- It told the passenger between 7 and 14 days before departure and offered a re-routing that leaves no
  more than 2 hours earlier and arrives no more than 4 hours later than planned.
- It told the passenger less than 7 days before departure and offered a re-routing that leaves no more than
  1 hour earlier and arrives no more than 2 hours later than planned.
- The cancellation was caused by extraordinary circumstances, see the separate document on that exemption.

## Denied boarding

A passenger denied boarding against their will, for example because of overbooking, is entitled to the same
fixed compensation as for a cancellation. Passengers who volunteer to give up their seat are not covered.

## Care and assistance

Independently of compensation, the airline must provide meals, refreshments and communication, and hotel
accommodation with transfers when an overnight stay is needed. The passenger may also choose between a
refund of the ticket and re-routing. These rights apply even where compensation is not owed.
